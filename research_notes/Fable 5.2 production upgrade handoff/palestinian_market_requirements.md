# Palestinian market requirements for Fastship

Research cut-off: **2026-11-13**. Web sources were accessed on that date unless a publication/update date is stated. **Recommendation** means a proposed product or engineering decision, not a verified market fact. **Gaza**, **West Bank**, and **East Jerusalem** are separated only where the evidence supports a distinction; otherwise the difference remains an explicit gap. This is product research, not legal, tax, payments-regulatory, or market-size advice.

## 1. What language, localization, and address model should Fastship support?

### Takeaway

Fastship should treat complete Arabic/RTL journeys and a flexible pin-plus-written-directions address as launch requirements to validate in the field, rather than assuming that a postal address or translated checkout alone is sufficient. Expo provides locale, RTL, and `Intl` building blocks, while the UPU documents a postal format—not evidence of courier-address coverage or landmark usage. — [Expo localization guide](https://docs.expo.dev/guides/localization/); [UPU Palestine addressing sheet, dated 05/2025](https://www.upu.int/UPU/media/upu/PostalEntitiesFiles/addressingUnit/pseEn.pdf)

### Cited Findings

- Expo's localization guide covers reading device locales, declaring per-app supported locales, translating app metadata and permission text, handling RTL layouts and mixed-direction content, and using `Intl` for dates, numbers, monetary amounts, lists, units, and plurals. It also warns that `Intl` formats values but does not itself tell the app which currency or units a user expects. — [Expo localization guide](https://docs.expo.dev/guides/localization/)
- The current Expo RTL section explicitly describes **SDK 58 and later** and says earlier versions had different defaults. Fastship currently declares `expo: ~57.0.31` and `expo-localization: ~57.0.2`, so SDK 58 defaults or configuration examples must not be assumed to describe this repository. — [Expo localization guide](https://docs.expo.dev/guides/localization/); [Fastship `package.json`](../../package.json)
- The Universal Postal Union's Palestine sheet specifies an **eight-character alphanumeric postcode**, placed to the right of the locality, and gives worked examples with addressee, locality, thoroughfare, town, locality plus postcode, and country. Its address-position guidance is for mail. — [UPU Palestine addressing sheet, dated 05/2025; page published 2025-06-02](https://www.upu.int/UPU/media/upu/PostalEntitiesFiles/addressingUnit/pseEn.pdf)
- The same UPU sheet contains Jerusalem and Abu Dis examples, but it does not measure how many homes or businesses have a complete courier-usable street address, how often postcodes are used, or whether a given map/geocoder can resolve them. — [UPU Palestine addressing sheet](https://www.upu.int/UPU/media/upu/PostalEntitiesFiles/addressingUnit/pseEn.pdf)

### Inferences

- **Recommendation — Arabic-first, not Arabic-only by assumption:** provide human-reviewed Arabic across customer, courier, dispatcher, notification, error, empty, offline, payment, cash, receipt, and support states. Keep English as a supported secondary language only if owner/user interviews justify it for launch; do not infer language shares from this research. Base locale handling and formatting on the cited Expo capabilities. — [Expo localization guide](https://docs.expo.dev/guides/localization/)
- **Recommendation — test direction, not just translation:** exercise full RTL navigation, back gestures, icons, validation, long names, truncated text, mixed Arabic/English addresses, phone numbers such as `+970…`, order IDs, and currency amounts. Prefer logical start/end layout behavior where supported, and verify Fastship's actual SDK 57 behavior in a development build rather than copying SDK 58 defaults. — [Expo localization guide](https://docs.expo.dev/guides/localization/); [Fastship `package.json`](../../package.json)
- **Recommendation — preserve user-entered meaning:** store names, landmarks, and directions as entered; do not destructively transliterate Arabic into Latin text or overwrite an Arabic address with a geocoder result. Display a normalized/geocoded suggestion separately and require confirmation before changing the delivery location.
- **Recommendation — hybrid address record:** support service area, governorate/city/locality, optional street/building/floor/apartment, optional postcode, an optional confirmed map pin plus capture accuracy/time, landmark, free-form directions, contact name, primary phone, optional alternate contact, and courier notes. The UPU format informs the optional postal fields; it does not justify making every field mandatory. — [UPU Palestine addressing sheet](https://www.upu.int/UPU/media/upu/PostalEntitiesFiles/addressingUnit/pseEn.pdf)
- **Recommendation — permissive validation with operational confirmation:** require an enabled service area, a reachable contact, and enough confirmed location information for dispatch. Do not require a postcode, formal street address, or GPS permission until field evidence shows that couriers can work reliably with that requirement. Offer manual pin placement and written directions when location permission is denied or a pin is inaccurate.
- **Recommendation — regional configuration:** keep service-area and address-validation rules configurable per operating area. Do not infer East Jerusalem or Gaza address coverage from the UPU's postal examples, and do not describe the hybrid model as a statistically established national norm before the pilot.

### Gaps

- Which languages and dialect wording do customers, merchants, couriers, dispatchers, and support staff actually prefer in each proposed service area? Which terms do they use for order, delivery, cash collection, refund, and reconciliation states?
- In a representative set of real deliveries, what proportion has a usable street address, postcode, accurate pin, landmark, or only verbal directions? The UPU source cannot answer this.
- How accurate are the chosen map tiles, search, reverse geocoding, and pin placement in each West Bank pilot neighborhood, East Jerusalem, and Gaza? No reliable comparative evidence was established here.
- Is landmark-plus-directions addressing common in Fastship's exact merchant/courier workflows? This remains a **field hypothesis**, not a cited prevalence claim.
- Owner interviews should capture five anonymized successful and five problematic recent deliveries per participating merchant/courier, including the original address text and how the courier actually found the destination; this qualitative sample would inform design, not estimate population prevalence.

## 2. What connectivity and device constraints should shape ordering and courier work?

### Takeaway

The strongest regional evidence supports designing for intermittent and inconsistent connectivity, with especially cautious assumptions for Gaza; it does not support a single network-quality claim for all Palestinian markets. Offline work must be visibly pending rather than presented as server-confirmed. — [ITU Resolution 18 (Rev. Baku, 2025)](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-d/opb/res/D-RES-D.18-2025-PDF-E.pdf)

### Cited Findings

- ITU Resolution 18 (Rev. Baku, 2025) notes damage to telecommunications infrastructure, repercussions of war particularly in Gaza, inconsistent mobile services inside Palestinian territories, and a continuing need to expand 4G/5G. It specifically says Gaza networks still operate using 2G technology and calls for urgent reconstruction and assistance. — [ITU Resolution 18 (Rev. Baku, 2025)](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-d/opb/res/D-RES-D.18-2025-PDF-E.pdf)
- The ITU resolution calls for assessments of the impact of telecommunications/ICT projects in both Gaza and the West Bank; it does not provide neighborhood-level throughput, latency, outage-frequency, or carrier coverage measurements for Fastship. — [ITU Resolution 18 (Rev. Baku, 2025)](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-d/opb/res/D-RES-D.18-2025-PDF-E.pdf)
- OCHA's 16 July 2026 situation report describes continuing military operations, movement restrictions, insecurity, displacement, and constraints on humanitarian operations in Gaza, and reports insecurity and access restrictions in the West Bank including East Jerusalem. This is humanitarian context, not a measurement of commercial delivery feasibility. — [UN OCHA Humanitarian Situation Report, 2026-07-16](https://www.ochaopt.org/content/humanitarian-situation-report-16-july-2026)

### Inferences

- **Recommendation — explicit customer order states:** an offline-created order is a local draft labeled **Not sent**. It may move through **Sending** to **Submitted** only after the server acknowledges the order and returns its server identifier. Failure or timeout returns to a retryable **Not sent / awaiting confirmation** state; the UI must never show false server acceptance.
- **Recommendation — duplicate-safe submission:** create a stable client operation ID before the first attempt, persist it across retries and app restarts, and have the server deduplicate retries. A timeout is an unknown outcome requiring status reconciliation, not automatic proof that the order failed or succeeded.
- **Recommendation — bounded courier offline work:** cache only the courier's assigned job snapshot and minimum necessary contact/address details. Queue status events with operation ID, local timestamp, and sequence; synchronize them idempotently; display **Recorded on device / pending sync** until acknowledged. Surface conflicts for dispatcher review rather than silently applying last-write-wins.
- **Recommendation — separate local recording from server truth:** offline delivery or cash collection may be recorded as pending sync, but must not be displayed as server-confirmed delivery, settled payment, or completed reconciliation. Disable or clearly degrade live tracking, ETA promises, and real-time support chat when connectivity is unavailable.
- **Recommendation — low-bandwidth payload policy:** keep core order/status payloads small, retry with bounded exponential backoff, avoid requiring image upload for core completion, and queue any optional proof photo as a compressed separate operation. Test airplane mode, packet loss, high latency, session expiry during reconnect, duplicate responses, process death, and clock changes.
- **Recommendation — Gaza caution:** do not promise real-time behavior or broad Gaza availability based on a West Bank test. Require a separate operational feasibility and network pilot there, informed by the ITU's Gaza-specific evidence. — [ITU Resolution 18](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-d/opb/res/D-RES-D.18-2025-PDF-E.pdf)
- **Recommendation — West Bank and East Jerusalem caution:** test actual carriers, neighborhoods, indoor locations, and working hours instead of treating “West Bank,” “East Jerusalem,” or “Palestine” as uniform coverage categories. No East Jerusalem-specific telecom rule is asserted here.
- **Recommendation — device acceptance from evidence:** measure the pilot users' actual OS versions, memory/storage constraints, battery behavior, and app-kill/restart patterns, then include representative lower-capability physical devices in acceptance testing. Do not claim Android/iOS market shares or a representative device tier without data.

### Gaps

- What are current real-world latency, throughput, outage duration, SMS/data reachability, and indoor coverage by carrier and neighborhood in the proposed West Bank areas, East Jerusalem, and Gaza?
- Which devices, OS versions, storage limits, battery-saving settings, and browser/WebView versions do target customers and couriers actually use? No reliable current device-distribution evidence was found.
- How long must a courier be able to work without connectivity, and which actions may safely remain local during that period? This is an owner operations decision as well as a technical question.
- Are offline maps required, or are cached address text, contact actions, and a previously loaded pin sufficient? Test this with couriers before buying or building an offline-map capability.
- A Reuters search result concerning West Bank 4G approval was not successfully retrieved in full and is not used as evidence. In any event, an approval would not by itself prove deployed coverage or service quality.

## 3. Which payment, currency, cash, and reconciliation capabilities are appropriate?

### Takeaway

Palestine has documented instant-payment, wallet, and electronic bill-payment infrastructure, but the existence of those services does not establish that Fastship can obtain a suitable merchant API or serve every region. Fastship should support cash only as an explicit operational workflow and integrate no more than one digital payment route at launch after verifying onboarding, regional reach, refunds, settlement, and reconciliation. — [PMA payment-system catalogue](https://www.pma.ps/payment-systems-details); [PalPay company overview](https://www.palpay.ps/en/who-we-are); [E-SADAD overview](https://www.ppsc.ps/en/e-sadad)

### Cited Findings

- The Palestine Monetary Authority's catalogue describes iBuraq as instant-transfer infrastructure between banks and financial institutions, E-SADAD as centralized electronic billing/payment, the National Key 194 as a card network linking ATMs and POS devices, and other national payment systems. The catalogue is not merchant-integration documentation for Fastship. — [PMA payment-system catalogue](https://www.pma.ps/payment-systems-details)
- Arab Islamic Bank's iBuraq page says its customers can send and receive instant transfers through that bank's online/mobile channels, including transfers to accounts at other local banks and to electronic wallets. It lists **JOD, USD, ILS, and EUR**, QR-based transfers, and wallet-number/IBAN routes. These are AIB's documented service features, not proof of a general Fastship checkout API or zero permanent fees. — [AIB iBuraq page, updated 2026-02-26](https://aib.ps/en/content/individual-services/771.html)
- PalPay says its Mahfazati wallet launched with Palestine Monetary Authority licensing in 2020 and lists POS and e-commerce solutions among its offerings. The page does not by itself establish current merchant eligibility, API terms, coverage, settlement reports, or refund behavior. — [PalPay company overview](https://www.palpay.ps/en/who-we-are)
- E-SADAD describes online bill payment through banking apps and wallets, recurring-invoice storage, detailed bill information, and electronic reconciliations. Its positioning as a bill-payment system should not be silently reinterpreted as a ready-made on-demand delivery checkout product. — [E-SADAD overview](https://www.ppsc.ps/en/e-sadad)

### Inferences

- **Recommendation — money model:** store every amount with an explicit currency; preserve order amount, delivery fee, discounts, expected collection, actual collection, refund, and settlement amounts separately. Never combine currencies in one balance or perform an implicit conversion. AIB's four-currency list proves the need to ask about currencies, not a requirement to launch all four. — [AIB iBuraq page](https://aib.ps/en/content/individual-services/771.html)
- **Recommendation — launch currencies:** select only the currencies used by the pilot merchants' pricing and settlement agreements. If conversion is later allowed, record the quoted rate, source, timestamp, original amount, converted amount, and who bears the difference; automatic FX is not a launch requirement.
- **Recommendation — payment-provider gate:** before selecting a provider, obtain production/sandbox API documentation and written answers for merchant onboarding, permitted business model, customer authentication, supported districts and customer account types, fees, settlement timing/currency, payment expiry, idempotency, server-to-server confirmation, full/partial refunds, failed/reversed payments, reconciliation exports, dispute handling, and support escalation.
- **Recommendation — independent payment states:** model `pending`, `authorized` if supported, `paid`, `failed`, `cancelled`, `refund_pending`, `refunded`, and `reconciliation_exception` as applicable. Never mark an order paid solely from a client redirect or local button press; require verified provider/server evidence and later reconcile against settlement records.
- **Recommendation — separate three ledgers:** fulfillment (`created → assigned → picked_up → delivered` or an exception), payment (`unpaid/pending → paid/failed/refunded`), and cash reconciliation (`due → collected → handed_in → reconciled` or an exception) must not be collapsed into one `status` field. A delivered order can still have an unresolved cash or refund exception.
- **Recommendation — cash collection if the pilot requires it:** record expected and actual amount/currency, collector, collection time, shortage/overpayment, refusal/failed collection reason, hand-in recipient/time, hand-in receipt/reference, discrepancy, adjustment, and final reconciler. Reconcile by courier, shift, currency, and hand-in batch; preserve an append-only audit trail for corrections.
- **Recommendation — cash receipt while offline:** label locally recorded collection **pending sync**, and do not treat it as handed in or reconciled. Require an explicit discrepancy workflow for expected-versus-actual amounts rather than editing the original amount to make totals balance.
- **Recommendation — narrow payment scope:** launch cash-on-delivery only if pilot merchants confirm the need and the ledger/hand-in controls pass. Add at most one digital method that passes the full provider gate and real refund/reconciliation tests; a cash-only pilot is preferable to an unverified payment integration. Do not claim that cash is dominant or that digital wallets are broadly preferred without field evidence.

### Gaps

- What proportion of Fastship's target orders do merchants and customers actually want paid by cash, bank transfer, wallet, card, or another method? No reliable current cash-dominance or payment-preference statistic was established.
- Which providers will contract with Fastship's exact legal entity and delivery business model, expose merchant APIs, and support the intended West Bank, East Jerusalem, or Gaza customers? Public product pages do not answer this.
- What are actual production fees, reserve/hold policies, settlement delays, refund limits, webhook reliability, reconciliation formats, and dispute service levels? These require vendor documents and sandbox/production verification.
- Which currencies must each merchant price, collect, refund, and settle in? Who supplies change for cash, and how are shortages, overpayments, partial deliveries, cancellations, and refunds handled?
- Does Fastship merely pass payment instructions to a licensed provider, or could any proposed flow cause it to hold customer/courier funds? Obtain local payments-regulatory advice before introducing stored balances, pooled funds, or delayed merchant payouts.

## 4. What onboarding, support, safety, receipt, and compliance workflows are needed?

### Takeaway

Use standards-based phone onboarding with a tested one-way OTP route, human-operable support/dispute workflows, minimal exposure of personal/location data, and operational receipts distinct from fiscal invoices. The available sources establish `+970` and important SMS constraints, but not universal numbering behavior, current comprehensive privacy law, or region-specific legal compliance. — [ITU numbering-plan contribution metadata](https://www.itu.int/md/T25-SG02-C-0145/en); [Twilio Palestine SMS guidelines](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/guidelines/ps/sms); [IFEX/7amleh privacy guide, 2023-06-14](https://ifex.org/a-procedural-guide-to-palestinian-personal-data-protection-online/)

### Cited Findings

- ITU Study Group 2 Contribution 145 is titled “Operational considerations related to the international use of the E.164 country code +970 and the updated National Numbering Plan of the State of Palestine.” The metadata dates the contribution 2025-12-22 and shows it posted 2026-01-09; the full contribution is TIES-restricted, so this research does not infer detailed number lengths or operator allocations from it. — [ITU SG02 Contribution 145 metadata](https://www.itu.int/md/T25-SG02-C-0145/en)
- Twilio's Palestine SMS guidelines list dialing code `+970`, **no two-way SMS support**, supported/preserved dynamic alphanumeric sender IDs, and best-effort international long-code delivery with the sender overwritten by a generic alphanumeric ID. These are Twilio-specific capabilities, not a guarantee for every provider or every destination. — [Twilio Palestine SMS guidelines, current page retrieved 2026-11-13](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/guidelines/ps/sms)
- A 2023 procedural guide published by IFEX and authored by 7amleh describes privacy protection under the Palestinian Basic Law and advocates dedicated personal-data-protection legislation. Its age and advocacy purpose mean it cannot establish the complete current 2026 legal position or Fastship's compliance obligations. — [IFEX/7amleh procedural guide, published 2023-06-14](https://ifex.org/a-procedural-guide-to-palestinian-personal-data-protection-online/)
- Search results also surfaced conflicting unsupported claims about later privacy legislation; without a verified current official text and professional interpretation, those claims are not adopted here. The 2023 guide is used only for the limited historical proposition above. — [IFEX/7amleh procedural guide](https://ifex.org/a-procedural-guide-to-palestinian-personal-data-protection-online/)

### Inferences

- **Recommendation — phone input:** accept and normalize E.164 numbers, default the country selector to `+970` where appropriate, but keep it editable and do not reject another legitimate prefix merely because the service is marketed to Palestinian users. Do not invent fixed national number lengths from the restricted ITU document. — [ITU SG02 Contribution 145 metadata](https://www.itu.int/md/T25-SG02-C-0145/en)
- **Recommendation — one verified OTP route:** use one-way SMS OTP only after delivery tests on the actual pilot carriers/regions. Add code expiry, resend cooldown, verification-attempt limits, generic anti-enumeration responses, abuse monitoring, and a support path for undelivered codes. Do not ask users to reply by SMS, because the cited Twilio route has no two-way support. — [Twilio Palestine SMS guidelines](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/guidelines/ps/sms)
- **Recommendation — no unverified fallback sprawl:** do not launch simultaneous SMS, voice, and messaging-app OTP fallbacks. Add a second route only if the first fails pilot acceptance and the replacement is independently verified. Provide manual review for merchants/couriers rather than silently bypassing verification.
- **Recommendation — role onboarding and access:** distinguish customer, merchant staff, courier, dispatcher, finance/reconciler, support, and administrator permissions. Require explicit approval for operational roles, least-privilege access to addresses/phones/cash records, session revocation, and an audit trail for sensitive changes.
- **Recommendation — usable support:** provide order-linked support categories for address/contact correction, unreachable recipient, unsafe/inaccessible stop, damaged/missing items, delivery disagreement, payment failure, cash discrepancy, cancellation, and refund. Preserve the order ID, current fulfillment/payment/reconciliation states, prior events, assigned owner, and next action; allow a support request to be drafted offline and marked pending sync.
- **Recommendation — human escalation:** publish actual support language, hours, expected response window, and escalation contact after the owner staffs them. Do not promise 24/7 real-time chat or rely on inbound SMS. Test dispatcher/customer/courier handoffs for duplicate orders, unknown sync outcomes, and disputed delivery.
- **Recommendation — courier and recipient safety:** let a courier flag unsafe or inaccessible delivery, contact dispatch, and record an exception without being forced to mark successful delivery. Limit precise live-location and recipient-contact access to authorized active operational needs, define when access ends, and avoid exposing historical precise location broadly in support screens.
- **Recommendation — proof without over-collection:** capture only the proof needed for the merchant's policy, with an exception path when a photo/signature cannot be obtained. Keep proof uploads separate from core offline completion and restrict their retention/access; do not make sensitive media a prerequisite for every order without field and legal review.
- **Recommendation — operational receipt:** show order/reference ID, merchant, item/delivery charges and discounts, total and currency, payment method and state, fulfillment state, cash collection/reconciliation state where relevant, event timestamps, refund/adjustment references, and support reference. If generated offline, label it as device-recorded/pending sync.
- **Recommendation — not automatically a fiscal invoice:** call this an **order/delivery receipt** until a qualified local adviser confirms the required invoice content and issuer. Do not add tax numbers, VAT claims, “tax invoice” wording, or fiscal-compliance assertions by assumption.
- **Recommendation — compliance workstream before launch:** have qualified advisers review the exact entity, contracts, consumer/cancellation/refund terms, courier relationship, payments flow, privacy/telemarketing/location rules, record retention, and tax-invoice duties for each intended jurisdiction/service area. Separately document data inventory, purposes, consent/permission flows, retention/deletion, role access, processors/subprocessors, cross-border hosting/support access, security controls, and incident response. This is a review checklist, not a conclusion about which law applies.

### Gaps

- What OTP delivery rate and latency does the selected provider achieve for each target carrier and region, including East Jerusalem and Gaza? What sender identity do recipients actually see, and what happens during delays or reordering?
- Which phone country codes and numbering patterns do Fastship's real East Jerusalem customers use? The research does not establish an East Jerusalem-specific numbering or telecom rule.
- What support languages, staffing, hours, escalation chain, service-level targets, refund authority, and safety procedures will the owner fund and operate?
- What identity/KYC information, if any, is required for each role and payment flow? Do not collect identity documents until the provider/legal review establishes necessity and retention.
- What current privacy, consumer, tax, electronic-transactions, courier/labor, and payments obligations apply to Fastship in each launch area? No current comprehensive official legal position was verified here.
- Which operational receipt fields and fiscal invoice fields are legally required, in which language, for how long, and by which entity? Keep these as professional-review questions.

## 5. What should launch include, what requires field validation, and what is out of scope?

### Takeaway

Launch should be a closed, measurable service-area pilot rather than a claim of uniform Palestinian coverage. The minimum coherent product joins localized ordering, flexible addresses, honest offline states, controlled cash/payment operations, human support, reconciliation, and legal review; additional regions and payment methods remain later expansion decisions. — [ITU Resolution 18](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-d/opb/res/D-RES-D.18-2025-PDF-E.pdf); [PMA payment-system catalogue](https://www.pma.ps/payment-systems-details)

### Cited Findings

- The ITU evidence differentiates Gaza's 2G/network-reconstruction context from broader Palestinian connectivity goals and also notes inconsistent mobile services; it does not justify enabling all regions under one reliability promise. — [ITU Resolution 18 (Rev. Baku, 2025)](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-d/opb/res/D-RES-D.18-2025-PDF-E.pdf)
- Public payment sources establish several possible rails and currencies but leave merchant access and operational integration unanswered, supporting a provider-verification gate rather than broad payment claims. — [PMA payment-system catalogue](https://www.pma.ps/payment-systems-details); [AIB iBuraq page, updated 2026-02-26](https://aib.ps/en/content/individual-services/771.html); [PalPay company overview](https://www.palpay.ps/en/who-we-are)
- Fastship already declares Expo localization and secure-storage dependencies, but dependency presence alone does not prove complete Arabic content, correct RTL behavior, secure session implementation, offline correctness, or market readiness. — [Fastship `package.json`](../../package.json)

### Inferences

- **Recommendation — initial launch boundary:** choose one explicitly listed service area only after merchant/courier/customer discovery and route/network testing. Enable one complete customer–merchant–courier–dispatcher loop; do not label the launch “West Bank,” “East Jerusalem,” “Gaza,” or “Palestine-wide” unless every included area has passed its own operational gates.
- **Recommendation — minimum launch capabilities:** human-reviewed Arabic and RTL; hybrid pin/landmark/contact/directions addresses; editable E.164 phone input with one tested OTP route; visible offline/not-sent/pending-sync states; duplicate-safe order/event submission; separate fulfillment, payment, and cash-reconciliation states; cash controls if required by pilot merchants; no more than one fully verified digital payment method; operational receipts; support/dispute/safety exceptions; role-based access and audit records.
- **Recommendation — rollout gates:**
  1. **Discovery:** document real addresses, payment preferences, currencies, devices, languages, support expectations, cash hand-in, and recent delivery failures without treating a qualitative sample as a population statistic.
  2. **Vendor/professional verification:** test the chosen OTP and payment routes; review contracts, settlement/refund/reconciliation files, privacy/security obligations, consumer terms, and tax-invoice requirements.
  3. **Closed pilot:** use invited merchants/couriers and known service boundaries; reconcile every order and cash/payment exception daily; record failed SMS, duplicate orders, sync conflicts, address corrections, support response times, and cash differences.
  4. **Limited production:** enable only tested service areas and payment/currency combinations, with named operations owners and rollback/disable procedures for OTP, payments, and new order intake.
  5. **Expansion:** reassess each additional West Bank area, East Jerusalem, and Gaza independently against address, network, payment, support, safety, and legal gates.
- **Recommendation — acceptance evidence:** demonstrate Arabic mixed-direction journeys on representative physical devices; offline draft/restart/retry without duplicates; unknown-timeout reconciliation; courier conflict handling; successful and failed OTP; real digital payment plus failure, cancellation, full/partial refund if offered, and settlement match; cash shortage/hand-in/discrepancy reconciliation; unsafe/inaccessible delivery escalation; and a receipt/support audit trail.
- **Recommendation — field-research questions for merchants:** Which neighborhoods are served? What address text actually arrives? Which orders fail and why? Which payment methods/currencies are requested? Who owns refunds and cash discrepancies? What settlement/report is needed? Which receipt/invoice is given today? What support hours and exceptions matter?
- **Recommendation — field-research questions for couriers/dispatchers:** How is a destination actually found? When are pins wrong or connectivity unavailable? Which job details must remain available offline? How are recipient contact, unsafe stops, failed delivery, cash change, hand-in, and disputed completion handled? Which Arabic/English labels are unambiguous?
- **Recommendation — field-research questions for customers/support/finance:** What language and contact channel are preferred? What OTP/payment failures occur? What order/payment/refund proof resolves disputes? Which personal details should be hidden? How quickly must support respond? Which daily courier/shift/currency/provider reports reconcile cash and digital settlement?
- **Recommendation — explicit launch non-goals:** simultaneous multi-region coverage; multiple payment gateways/wallets/cards; automatic FX or pooled/stored balances; two-way SMS support; unverified voice/messaging OTP fallbacks; guaranteed live tracking or ETA; custom navigation/offline maps; a tax engine or self-declared fiscal invoice; unattended fully automated dispute resolution; and claims of legal compliance, universal coverage, device representativeness, cash dominance, or landmark prevalence without evidence.
- **Recommendation — owner decisions required before implementation:** exact service area; participating merchants/couriers; Arabic/English scope; accepted currencies; cash policy; selected OTP/payment vendors; offline action limits; support hours/authority; location/proof retention; refund/cancellation policy; reconciliation owner; and local legal/tax reviewers.

### Gaps

- No statistically representative market survey was found or conducted, so this research cannot rank payment methods, estimate cash usage, describe device composition, quantify incomplete addresses, or forecast adoption.
- The optimal first service area remains undecided. It must be selected from actual merchant demand and field evidence, not from the convenience of available public sources.
- Current East Jerusalem-specific service, telecom, payment, tax, and regulatory differences remain insufficiently evidenced. Treat East Jerusalem as a separate verification track rather than either identical to the West Bank or automatically excluded.
- Gaza's commercial delivery feasibility, reachable merchants/couriers, payment reach, addressing, support, safety, and network performance need separate current field assessment; the cited humanitarian and ITU evidence does not answer those commercial questions.
- Current provider contracts/APIs and current authoritative legal/tax interpretations are missing. These are launch dependencies, not reasons to invent product facts.
- The final production scope and acceptance thresholds require owner approval after the field pilot. This note deliberately supplies a narrow proposed baseline, not an assertion that the proposed workflow has already been validated in Palestine.
