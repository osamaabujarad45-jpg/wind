## What public Uber sources actually document about matching, ETA, live location, cancellation, safety, and proof of delivery

### Takeaway
Uber publicly documents the *shape* of the product promises — matching that optimizes rider wait and driver earnings, ETAs refined by ML on top of a routing engine, push rather than poll for live trip state, time-boxed cancellation fees, PIN/RideCheck/share-trip safety tools, and PIN/photo/signature/ID proof of delivery — but not the operational thresholds (offer expiry seconds, ETA MAE, end-to-end availability, dispatch latency). "Uber-quality" therefore has to be reconstructed as observable behavior plus independently sourced platform thresholds, not copied from a published Uber SLO.

### Cited Findings
- Uber describes ETA as a hybrid: a routing engine produces a road-graph ETA, then an ML post-processing model (DeepETA) predicts the residual using origin/destination, time, traffic, and request type; the model was launched for global 4-wheel ETA prediction and must return "within a few milliseconds at most" — [Uber, DeepETA (2022)](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/deepeta-how-uber-predicts-arrival-times). The post states MAE must "improve significantly" over XGBoost but publishes no MAE figure.
- Uber's matching is explicitly multi-objective: "minimizing wait times for riders while maximizing earnings for drivers", with a reinforcement-learning value function used as a ranking signal; Uber reports a 0.52% driver-earnings increase and a 2.2% reduction in rider cancellations in switchback experiments, deployed in 400+ cities — [Uber, RL for Marketplace Balance (2025)](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/reinforcement-learning-for-modeling-marketplace-balance). This is self-reported, not independently audited.
- Uber moved trip state from polling to push because "at some point, 80% of requests made to the backend API gateway were polling calls"; polling caused battery drain and retries on 2G/3G/spotty networks; the push platform (RAMEN) used SSE then gRPC, at-least-once delivery, 4-second heartbeats, 7-second client timeout, TTL per message, and priority buckets — [Uber, Real-Time Push Platform (2020)](https://www.uber.com/ng/en/blog/real-time-push-platform). The same post reports 99.99% server-side reliability, 1.5M concurrent connections and 250,000 messages/second — an infrastructure figure, not an end-to-end rider SLO.
- Uber's live-location sharing for pickup is time- and proximity-bounded: "Live location sharing is turned on, by default, for riders and drivers during the last three minutes of the pickup", only when the driver is close and the ETA is under 3 minutes, and the rider can turn it off — [Uber Help, Sharing your pickup location (iOS)](https://help.uber.com/en/riders/article/sharing-your-pickup-location-ios).
- Uber's cancellation policy is time-windowed and product-specific: for most economy ride types a rider fee applies if the rider cancels 2+ minutes after requesting; for most premium types, 5+ minutes; no fee if the driver is still dropping off another rider or is not making progress — [Uber Help, How rider cancellation fees are charged](https://help.uber.com/en/driving-and-delivering/article/how-rider-cancellation-fees-are-charged); [Uber Help, Cancellation fees explained](https://help.uber.com/en/riders/article/cancellation-fees-explained). Drivers may receive the standard fee or actual time/distance toward pickup, whichever is more — [Uber Help, Request cancellation fee](https://help.uber.com/en/driving-and-delivering/article/request-cancellation-fee).
- Uber's rider safety stack publicly includes: driver/vehicle details before the ride; optional 4-digit PIN that the driver must enter before starting, with 5 attempts — [Uber, How PIN verification works (2019)](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/pin-verification-drivers); GPS trip tracking; Share My Trip; RideCheck detecting unexpected long stops, off-course travel, or a possible crash; in-app emergency button that displays live location and trip details to the dispatcher — [Uber, Our commitment to safety](https://www.uber.com/us/en/safety/our-commitment); [Uber, Rider safety](https://www.uber.com/us/en/ride/safety); [Uber, Driver safety](https://www.uber.com/us/en/drive/safety).
- Uber Eats documents PIN handoff as a delivery-completion control: a 4-digit PIN shown to the customer, entered by the delivery person; if the customer does not know it, the last 4 digits of the phone number are the fallback — [Uber Help, PIN code authentication](https://help.uber.com/en/ubereats/restaurants/article/pin-code-authentication). Uber's merchant guidance calls PIN "one of the most fraud-proof" proof-of-delivery methods and also lists photos and electronic signatures — [Uber Eats Merchant, Proof of Delivery](https://merchants.ubereats.com/us/en/resources/articles/proof-of-delivery).
- Uber Direct's developer docs expose pickup and dropoff verification methods: barcode, photo, signature, ID check, and pincode; pincode is dropoff-only and can be combined with other methods; leave-at-door deliveries auto-enable photo proof — [Uber Developers, Proof of delivery](https://developer.uber.com/docs/deliveries/guides/proof-of-delivery); [Uber Developers, Pincode](https://developer.uber.com/docs/deliveries/guides/pincode). Photos are retained 7 days in the Dashboard and available 30 days via the API — [Uber Developers, Proof of delivery](https://developer.uber.com/docs/deliveries/guides/proof-of-delivery).

### Inferences
- A defensible "Uber-quality" definition is behavioral, not algorithmic: match quickly with a bounded offer and a fallback; show an ETA that is refreshed and honest about degradation; show a live position that is actually seconds old and explicitly marks staleness; state cancellation terms before booking and enforce them consistently; put identity verification, share-trip, and a staffed emergency path one tap away; require proof of handoff for delivery; and never silently lose an order or a payment.
- Uber's public numbers cannot be transplanted as acceptance criteria: the 99.99% figure is server-side push infrastructure from 2020, the ETA post gives no error target, and no public source states offer-expiry seconds, dispatch p95, or an end-to-end availability SLO. Any such number in a Fastship acceptance matrix must be labeled **proposed**, with the measurement method named.
- Uber's own direction of travel — push over polling, ML residual ETA, marketplace-level matching — implies that a local app without traffic data, without dispatch, and without telemetry cannot claim parity on matching or ETA, even if its screens look similar.

### Gaps
- No public Uber document found that states: dispatch offer TTL in seconds, maximum offers per round, search timeout, dispatch p95 latency, ETA MAE/p90 error, location-fix end-to-end freshness, crash-free-session target, or end-to-end availability SLO. These are not published; do not attribute them to Uber.
- No independent benchmark of Uber's ETA accuracy or matching outcomes was found; the 0.52%/2.2% figures are Uber's own switchback results.
- Uber's safety pages describe features, not response-time commitments or staffing levels; no public SOS acknowledgement SLA was found.

## Defensible thresholds: what is published, what is proposed, and why

### Takeaway
Only a handful of numbers are genuinely published and citable: Google Play's user-perceived crash/ANR bad-behavior thresholds, Android/iOS background-location limits, Uber's PIN attempts and cancellation windows, Uber's 99.99% push-infrastructure reliability, and Google SRE's error-budget method. Everything else — location freshness, marker latency, ETA error, dispatch expiry, API p95, map-tile recovery — has to be a **proposed** target derived from user-visible failure modes and then measured. A credible bar uses dual thresholds (typical and tail), a stated window, and a named measurement point.

### Cited Findings
- Google Play's bad-behavior thresholds are 1.09% of daily active users with a user-perceived crash and 0.47% with a user-perceived ANR, averaged across devices, with an 8% per-device-model threshold — [Android Developers, Crashes](https://developer.android.com/google/play/vitals/crash); [Android Developers, ANRs](https://developer.android.com/google/play/vitals/anr). These are store-visibility floors, not a quality target; a 99.9% crash-free-session target is stricter and is **proposed**, not published by Google or Uber.
- Without a foreground service, Android 8.0+ throttles background location to "only a few times each hour" — [Android Developers, Background location limits](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/oreo/background-location-limits). On Android 14+, a `location` foreground service must declare the type and hold `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`/`ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION`; starting it from the background additionally requires `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION` — [Android Developers, Foreground service types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types). This makes "3-second updates while backgrounded" a platform-permission claim, not a code claim.
- iOS continues background location only when `allowsBackgroundLocationUpdates` is true and updates were started in the foreground; the OS may still suspend background apps to preserve battery — [Apple, allowsBackgroundLocationUpdates](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/allowsbackgroundlocationupdates); [Apple, Handling location updates in the background](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/handling-location-updates-in-the-background).
- Google SRE recommends SLIs as good events / total events, a rolling four-week window, error budgets, and multiple latency thresholds to capture the tail (e.g., 90% < 100 ms and 99% < 400 ms) — [Google SRE Workbook, Implementing SLOs](https://sre.google/workbook/implementing-slos/). A 99.9% availability SLO over 30 days permits 43.2 minutes of full outage (arithmetic), and over 3M requests permits 3,000 failed requests — the worked example is in the same chapter.
- OWASP states that authentication tokens, session IDs, JWTs and refresh tokens must not be stored in `localStorage`/`sessionStorage`, because any XSS in the origin can read them; it recommends HttpOnly/Secure/SameSite cookies or a BFF, server-side invalidation on logout, and idle plus absolute timeouts — [OWASP Session Management Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html).
- Uber's documented PIN control is 4 digits and 5 attempts, and the trip cannot start until the correct PIN is entered — [Uber, How PIN verification works](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/pin-verification-drivers). This is a concrete, low-cost bar Fastship can copy for rides, not only parcels.
- Uber's documented cancellation window is 2 minutes for economy and 5 minutes for premium after request, with exceptions for a driver still dropping off or not progressing — [Uber Help, How rider cancellation fees are charged](https://help.uber.com/en/driving-and-delivering/article/how-rider-cancellation-fees-are-charged). This is the closest thing to a published cancellation UX bar; the fee amount is market-specific.

### Proposed thresholds (explicitly **PROPOSED**, not Uber-published)

| Criterion | Proposed pass threshold | Measurement point | Why this number |
|---|---|---|---|
| Auth success latency | p95 ≤ 1.5 s end-to-end on 3G; server handler p95 ≤ 500 ms | Client request start → session token stored; server access log | Login is the first impression; 1.5 s is a common mobile interaction budget, not an Uber figure |
| Auth failure indistinguishability | Unknown identifier, wrong password and blocked account return the same status and body; p95 timing delta ≤ 150 ms | API response + synthetic timing probe | Prevents account enumeration; the repo already returns a generic `wrong_credentials` |
| Session revocation | Every device gets 401 on its next request within 5 s of logout, password reset, role/status change, or block | API probe with two tokens | OWASP requires server-side invalidation; 5 s covers one request cycle |
| Session lifetime | Idle ≤ 30 days customer, ≤ 7 days driver; absolute ≤ 90 days | Token table timestamps | Repo uses 30/90 for all roles; drivers handling cash should be shorter |
| Location freshness (moving) | p95 fix age ≤ 5 s device→server→viewer; p99 ≤ 10 s | Driver timestamp at fix, server receipt, viewer render | 3 s native capture with a 5 s moving upload floor + network; 5 s is the smallest defensible "live" claim on 3G |
| Location freshness (stationary) | p95 ≤ 15 s; explicit stale label at 30 s; hide at 120 s | Same | Matches 15 s stationary cadence and the repo's 120 s server cutoff |
| Marker update latency | p95 ≤ 1 s (4G) / ≤ 3 s (3G) from viewer receiving a newer fix to marker animation start; p99 ≤ 5 s | Client instrumentation around `setLngLat`/animation frame | Uber's push architecture exists to avoid poll-interval lag; 1 s is a proposed visual bar |
| ETA error | p50 absolute error ≤ 2 min or 20% (whichever is larger) for trips < 15 min; p90 ≤ 5 min; signed bias within ±10% | 50+ completed trips with GPS ground truth | No public Uber MAE; this is a product-acceptance bar, must be measured locally |
| ETA coverage | ≥ 95% of live views with a fix < 30 s show a numeric ETA; 100% show an explicit "no estimate" state otherwise | Client telemetry + API logs | Prevents silent blank ETA; repo already returns `null` on no route |
| Dispatch offer expiry | Offer TTL 15 s; max 3 offers per round; search timeout 90 s; an expired offer can never be accepted | Server timestamps + acceptance attempt | These are the repo's own configured values; proposed because Uber does not publish them |
| Cancellation policy | Free-cancel window 2 min economy / 5 min premium; after accept, driver compensated; policy shown before booking | Product copy + ledger | Mirrors Uber's published windows; amounts remain local policy |
| SOS acknowledgement | Human acknowledgement p95 ≤ 60 s, 24/7; incident row and location attached ≤ 5 s | On-call log + incident timestamp | A push to an admin is not a response; 60 s is a proposed staffing bar |
| Crash-free sessions | ≥ 99.9% crash-free sessions; user-perceived crash rate < 1.09% and ANR < 0.47% (store floor) | Crash SDK / Play vitals | 99.9% proposed; 1.09%/0.47% published by Google |
| API availability | 99.9% over rolling 28 days (43.2 min full outage); error rate budget 0.1% | Load balancer/server metrics | Google SRE method; 99.9% is a proposed starting SLO |
| API latency | Read p95 < 300 ms, write p95 < 500 ms, p99 < 1 s at 60 concurrent users | Server histogram; client probe | Repo's local gate uses 500/1000 ms; propose tightening after measurement |
| Map first tile | p95 ≤ 3 s on 4G; explicit failure state ≤ 20 s; retry success ≥ 99% after network restore | Client `sourcedata`/`error` timestamps | Repo already has a 20 s failure timer; 3 s is a proposed visual bar |
| Push offer delivery | p95 ≤ 5 s, p99 ≤ 15 s from offer creation to device receipt while foregrounded | Push provider receipt + client log | Push is the dispatch path; a 15 s offer with 15 s delivery is useless |
| Location upload success | ≥ 99.5% of fixes accepted while foreground/FGS; queued fixes replayed within 60 s of reconnect | Server `accepted` receipts + client queue | Prevents silent gaps at checkpoints/tunnels |

### Inferences
- Use two numbers per criterion: a typical (p50/p95) and a tail (p99) or a coverage percentage. A single average hides the failure users remember — the SRE workbook makes the same point with 90%/99% latency thresholds.
- Measure at the user-visible point, not only the server: "fix age at viewer render" and "marker animation start" are the SLIs that match the promise; server receipt time alone can look healthy while the phone shows a stale car.
- Treat the repo's configured values (15 s offer, 3 offers, 90 s search, 30 s matching freshness, 120 s public cutoff) as **candidate** thresholds to validate in the field, not as proof of Uber parity.

### Gaps
- No published Uber end-to-end availability, ETA MAE, dispatch latency, location freshness, or crash-free target was found. Any table row claiming "Uber does X" for those is unsupported.
- The proposed numbers are engineering judgment, not statistically derived from local data; they should be baselined during a pilot and then frozen as SLOs with an error-budget policy.
- No local road-speed or carrier dataset exists yet to compute the proposed ETA error or location freshness distributions.

## What the repository actually implements — and what is only asserted

### Takeaway
The repo has a genuinely careful request/auth/tracking core: generic login failures, per-identity and per-IP throttles, server-side session revocation, hashed share tokens, a public tracking view with an explicit field allowlist, a 120 s freshness cutoff, a push-style long-poll for fixes, ETA bound to driver and trip stage, SOS that freezes the trip, and a 5-attempt handover code. It also has real gaps that the UI cannot hide: real dispatch is compiled off in production, the default deployment is single-instance SQLite with no HA, web sessions live in `localStorage` for 90 days, there is no crash/performance telemetry, rides have no rider-verification PIN, there is no RideCheck/off-course detection, and ETA is OSRM duration with no traffic model or accuracy measurement.

## West Bank operating context — where the bar changes

### Takeaway
The West Bank changes the bar in four ways: movement is dominated by 925 documented obstacles and frequently closed checkpoints, not by traffic; mobile data is slower and less uniform than Uber's benchmark markets; cash is the default settlement; and addresses are pins/landmarks rather than street numbers. A credible local "Uber-quality" bar must therefore weight closure-aware ETA, offline tolerance, cash reconciliation, and Arabic/RTL at least as heavily as matching sophistication.

### Cited Findings
- As of December 2025, OCHA documented 925 movement obstacles in the West Bank including East Jerusalem — 43% above the 20-year average — including 89 staffed 24/7 checkpoints, 218 partial checkpoints, 232 road gates (159 frequently closed), 114 linear closures, 167 earthmounds and 105 roadblocks — [OCHA, Movement and Access in the West Bank, April 2026](https://www.ochaopt.org/content/movement-and-access-west-bank-april-2026). This is the dominant source of ETA variance.
- Mobile data in the West Bank has historically been 3G-only with median speeds around 4–5 Mbps, an order of magnitude below Israel's median — [ts2.tech, Internet Access in Palestine (2025)](https://ts2.tech/en/internet-access-in-palestine-west-bank-and-gaza); [Al-Shabaka, ICT in Palestine](https://al-shabaka.org/briefs/ict-in-palestine-challenging-power-dynamics-and-limitations). ### Inferences
- ETA accuracy cannot be judged on a free-flow model in this market: a 10-minute OSRM estimate can become 40 minutes at a closed checkpoint. The acceptance bar should include a "closure-aware ETA" slice: p90 error on trips that encounter a reported closure, not just the overall p90.
- Offline tolerance is not a nice-to-have: with 3G-class speeds and gaps, the driver app must queue fixes and replay them with timestamps, and the customer UI must say "last seen X ago" rather than freeze. Both the native delivery layer and the web path drop a fix after a network failure rather than replaying it — [src/location-delivery.ts:71](../../src/location-delivery.ts); [src/driver-work.tsx:271](../../src/driver-work.tsx) — which is the wrong default for this market.
- Cash-first means the cash ledger and driver remittance are core product, not a fallback; card PSP work can follow a pilot if corporate demand is not the beachhead.
- Arabic/RTL and pin/landmark addressing are already strengths; do not trade them for a generic Uber clone.
